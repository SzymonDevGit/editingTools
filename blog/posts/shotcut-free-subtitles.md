Shotcut, the free open-source editor, has had a **Subtitles** panel since version 22.12. It can import SRT files, show them on the timeline and burn them into the video. Pair it with a free SRT from Whisper and the whole workflow costs nothing.

## 1. Make the SRT

Open [AutoSubtitler](/), drop in your video and press **Make subtitles**. Download the .srt when it's done. The video never leaves your computer.

## 2. Open the Subtitles panel

In Shotcut, choose **View › Subtitles**. The panel lists subtitle tracks and their items.

## 3. Import the SRT

In the Subtitles panel's menu, choose **Import SRT** (sometimes shown as **Import from file**), pick the .srt, and confirm the language. A subtitle track appears on the timeline.

## 4. Edit

Select a subtitle in the panel to change its text, start or end. You can also adjust the timing on the timeline.

## 5. Burn in or keep separate

- To draw the subtitles into the picture, use **Burn In Subtitles** from the panel menu. This adds a text filter you can style with a font, size, colour and outline.
- If you export to a container that supports subtitle tracks, such as MKV, Shotcut can include them as a separate track that viewers switch on and off.
- You can also export the track back to an .srt file from the panel menu.

## Tips

- Shotcut's menu labels have changed a little between releases. If an item isn't where this guide says, look in the Subtitles panel's menu (the ☰ button).
- Using Linux? AutoSubtitler runs in any modern browser, so there's nothing to install.
